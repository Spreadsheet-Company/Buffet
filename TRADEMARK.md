# Trademark policy

"Buffet" is the name Spreadsheet Company holds for this game, and "all you
can sheet" is its tagline. The licenses in this repository (AGPL-3.0-or-later,
CC-BY-SA-4.0, 0BSD, Apache-2.0) grant rights in the *code and text*; they
grant no rights in the names or any logo. The code forks and the name does
not, which is why this file exists on the same day as the licenses, as
Alonzo's does. The name is a common noun, and a weaker mark than a coined
word; this policy does not pretend otherwise, and states the practice
anyway, so that it is written down from the first day of use. The name is
also a pun, and the pun's other half is a living person: the game keeps his
name, his face and his words out of it, and this policy claims nothing
about him.

## You may, without asking

- Use the names to refer to this game accurately: "a Buffet strategy
  sheet", "a scenario pack for Buffet", "plays Buffet 0.1".
- Keep the names the game itself defines, its sheets', its stations' and
  its licences', in a copy or a fork. They are part of how the game works,
  and this policy never asks you to rename them.
- Name a strategy, a scenario pack, a classroom assignment or a joke pack
  "for Buffet", provided it is clear you are not the project.
- Quote the names in reviews, articles, talks and academic work.

## Please ask first

- Using the name or the tagline, or a confusingly similar one, as the name
  of a modified game, a fork, a hosted copy, a company, a product or a
  domain. A fork is welcome under the licenses; it needs its own name.
- Using the names in a way that suggests the project endorses, certifies or
  supports your copy, product or service.
- Any use of a logo, once one exists.

Ask at english@spreadsheet.company.
