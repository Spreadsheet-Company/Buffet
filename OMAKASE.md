## The standing decisions: the kitchen's rules

*Numbered so an item can cite them. `BD-2`, `BD-4` and `BD-6` are the owner's words from `BUFFET.md`; the rest are proposed (rec), to be adopted, amended or struck by the owner at `COURSE.0`'s approval, and until then they bind this file's items and nothing else. The engine's rules cross the road under their own numbers and are never re-minted: `AD-6` (everything the engine holds is a cell), `AD-7` (the step computes values and never formulas), `SD-13` (no outbound call, ever), the `GENSYM` veto, and the kit's ratchet in Alonzo's `ROADMAP.md` under "The order, and why".*

- **`BD-1`. Every course is a game, and a course changes no course before it.** A course is served only when it is playable alone, from the exam to an ending, with its own replay golden, its own describe golden and the owner's hand test; and the course before it stays byte for byte what it was, proved by the pass expanding the earlier course's frozen source to its frozen cartridge (`MISE.1`). A course's bytes change only by a dated fix of its own, its replay re-recorded, never by a later course's build. So a playtester plays course 0, then course 1 as an expansion over it, and so on to 7, and every replay ever recorded keeps playing.
- **`BD-2`. Funny is a requirement (owner).** The game is fun insofar as it is funny, jokes all the way down. Every player-visible string is a row of a data sheet with a chance at a joke; the voice is `BUFFET.md` §2's deadpan compliance; the rules are its rules, punch up, fictional names, clarity first, strings as data, PG-13. A string with no joke has a reason written beside it.
- **`BD-3`. The market is exogenous, deterministic and open.** The market's path is a pure function of the seed, the difficulty and the scenario; the dice are a Park and Miller cell seeded from `Clock!B3` and nothing else is random; the player's orders never move a price (slippage is a cost, not a path); and the model is a sheet anyone may read, so the open-book problem of `BUFFET.md` §5 is a feature by design, and a score is alpha over a benchmark on the same seed (`CRITIC.1`), never a raw net worth.
- **`BD-4`. Checkers first, chess one license at a time (owner).** The first five minutes need nothing a license has not unlocked; each license is an exam and a layer of the interface; a mechanic that would frighten a first player stands behind a license and never on the opening screen. Progressive disclosure is the product.
- **`BD-5`. Toy models, labeled.** Every simplification is a row of the *Things this game gets wrong* sheet (`MENU.5`); the game is not advice and says so; no real data, no feed, no real ticker's price (`SD-13`); no real money, ever; a concept card is read by a finance educator before it ships, since "tutorial" implies correctness.
- **`BD-6`. Lovelace Lavender and nothing else (owner's standing rule for the house's games).** Gains and losses show by glyph and shade step, never by red and green; a semantic exception for the meaning of loss is the owner's to grant and is an open decision, not a rule.
- **`BD-7`. The market day is the market's clock, and only the calendar reads the frame.** One tick is one market day; prices, fees, timers and events move at a day's boundary and nowhere else; a person's act is answered at the next frame, inside the day. The Day sheet alone reads `Clock!B1` (`POT.1`); every other formula reads the day, so that a frame between boundaries recomputes nothing but what the person touched. Held by the step row's own count, `(step frame evaluated of ...)`, the cells an ordinary frame evaluates being the calendar's and what the person touched, and by `describe`'s census showing `(every-frame 0)`, no shape computed whole each frame.
- **`BD-8`. State is a cell, and the cartridge never writes code.** A position, a balance, a timer, a license is formula state over its twin (the kit's `count`, `hold` and `since`) or a value the Write sheet puts there (`put`), and never a thing the host keeps (`AD-6`); the cartridge's step writes values and never formulas (`AD-7`); the one place a formula enters at run time is a person's own act on the Strategy sheet (`RECIPE.1`), recorded by the replay as an edit. Strings are data; a picker is `CHOOSE` over references, generated by a macro.
- **`BD-9`. Two pockets, and no switch moves money.** The firm's capital and the partner's wealth are separate cells; the Personal/Firm selector changes which pocket an order funds and what the plate shows, and never a balance; every transfer, draw or contribution is a row of the ledger with the pocket it left and the pocket it entered.
- **`BD-10`. Nothing leaves the kitchen untasted.** The game written by hand in Rust holds every frame of every course (`MISE.2`); where mathematics has a closed form, the cells are held to it to the last bit or a stated tolerance; a course has a replay golden and a describe golden; the playtest pass (Alonzo's `ROADMAP.md`, the owner's name of 2026-10-12) runs before a course is served; an accelerator, a kit hoist or a language ask is made with a measurement in hand and never before.

## The order, and why

Each course is what makes the next cheap, and the instrument comes before the operation (Alonzo's `ROADMAP.md`'s rule; `Frazaro/docs/metameta/METAMETALISP4.md`'s microscope before the dissection). The sequence is `BUFFET.md` §13's, kept because each course isolates one cost for the engine and one decision for the player:

| Course | Name | The player learns | What it proves to the engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Open for business | buy, sell, wait, and that the weather turns | a model-shaped grid of short runs and text, a rate and a click mixed, the day clock, error values at overflow |
| 1 | Bonds and rates | duration, maturity, and that cash has a yield | timers over the twin, amortization with `^`, a chain over a curve |
| 2 | The firm | fees, clients, payroll, and the draw | `MAX` roll-ups over unlike rows, flows, a whole-graph event |
| 3 | Leverage and weather | margin, the forecast, dry powder | deterministic cascades through the Write sheet, locked rows, a market with state |
| 4 | Derivatives and the pane | payoff, probability, time decay | depth, a triangular graph twenty deep, a legs-by-grid block |
| 5 | The Strategy sheet | rules, backtests, and that they were programming | a person's formulas written at run time, replays of edits, a long seek |
| 6 | The rest of the menu | rotation across every class | data at scale, the procedural dial against the cap |
| 7 | Rivals, prestige, scenarios | styles, vintage, history | the scenario switch's whole frame, verification by re-execution |

**The measuring track runs first and beside every course.** `MISE.1` lays the kitchen out before a row is written, since the composition rule (`BD-1`) is cheap on day one and dear after; `MISE.2`'s game by hand grows with each course and is the oracle of every frame; `MISE.3` answers the four questions the first course hangs on (the window's cost, a number format with a glyph, the picker's limit, the boundary frame against the ordinary frame) in a day of measurement; the floors row enters at course 2, when there is a roll-up worth timing; the playtest session is written for course 0 and extended at every course after.

**What Buffet does not wait for.** Not `REPO.5`, the arcade: this game is a repository of its own since 2026-10-14, as Alonzo's charter has it for the games after the first, and the arcade's adjudication is the demos'. No network item: `REPO.6`, `ENGINE.11`, `ENGINE.12` and the station's live half are behind a gate this cartridge never needs; the leaderboard is a pull request and the classroom grades by re-execution. Not `ENGINE.8`'s second slice: course 0 draws from the full record at a measured cost and takes the values view (`KERNEL.37`, shipping in `vla-lang` 0.9.3) the day the pin moves. Not `KERNEL.8`'s lookups: `CHOOSE` over references spells every picker and ladder until `INDEX` computes, and the ladder's macro changes spelling in one place that day. Not `ENGINE.5`'s save, until course 7's careers ask for one.

**What Buffet asks, in order, and which course asks it** (`MISE.6` keeps the ledger): Alonzo's `REPO.4` for `alonzo` on crates.io at 0.1.0 (`HOUSE.2`, the pin every build waits on; a session of the owner's is publishing it); `KERNEL.37` for the window (course 0, by the pin's move); `ENGINE.9`'s cap decision (course 6); `ENGINE.13`'s seek for the backtest and offline progress (course 5; stepping at full rate is the fallback); `ENGINE.7` for rules edited without a reload (course 5, a convenience); `KERNEL.8` for the short spelling (course 6); `KERNEL.31` where a roll-up over a wide sheet measures dear (course 2 or 6); `ENGINE.5` for a saved career (course 7); `ENGINE.10` for a signed replay (course 7, the classroom's and the leaderboard's trust, not their mechanism); Frazaro's page hosting both modules for English rules and the export (courses 5 and 7, 🔒).

**The ratchet stands.** A pattern Buffet writes twice goes into Buffet's own library (`cartridges/kitchen.vla`, `MISE.1`); a pattern a second cartridge writes alike moves to Alonzo's kit (`common.vla`, by a pull request there, the copy under `kit/` following at the next pin), the cartridges then expanding to their bytes unchanged; a mechanic enters the engine or the language only on a measurement and a second client. The market generator, the ladder, the picker and the lattice are Buffet's own until another cartridge writes one.

**The playtest pass closes every course.** After the hand test: a scripted session of the course played as a person plays it (`MISE.5`), every frame held, each phase timed; the real host in Chrome beside it, clicks included, timed without a canvas; then `CART.1`'s route over what it finds, a profile, each candidate prototyped in scratch, an A/B, the findings filed as asks, fixes or a later course's mechanic.
