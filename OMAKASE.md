## The service: how to read this file

`BUFFET.md` §1 gave the game its kitchen: asset classes are *stations*, a *plate* is a portfolio, the *sneeze guard* is compliance, the salad bar is bonds and the dessert bar is crypto. This file keeps those names for the parts of the game, and adds the words a kitchen uses for the work itself, so that a reader who knows a restaurant knows the roadmap:

- **The house** is the repository: its terms, its names, its pins, its releases.
- **Mise en place** is everything in its place before the doors open: the kit copied, the layout decided, the first measurements taken. It is also the instruments the kitchen keeps beside the line while it cooks: the game written by hand, the floors, the playtest session.
- **A course** is a version. Eight courses, 0 to 7, each a game a diner can play alone from the exam to an ending, each composed on the course before it and changing nothing of it (`BD-1`). A course served is a release (`HOUSE.5`).
- **A dish** is an item: one mechanic, built for one course. A dish served across courses is built in **slices**, one a course, and each course's list names the slice it takes.
- **The pass** is where a dish is checked before it leaves the kitchen, and the word is Alonzo's own: the pass expands a source into the cartridge the engine loads (`SPEC.md` §7.8). Nothing goes to the table that has not been through it, the game by hand holding every frame and the checks green.
- **The tasting** is a course's oracle and the owner's hand test. **The playtest pass** is the course played as a person plays it, every frame held and each phase timed.
- **Service** is the release: the tag, the notes, the page deployed.
- **The spike** is where a finished ticket goes, `SPIKE.md`.
- **The ratchet** is how a pattern moves. Written twice in Buffet, it goes into Buffet's own library, `src/kitchen.vla`. Written alike by a second cartridge, it goes into Alonzo's kit by a pull request there, the copy, `src/common.vla`, following at the next pin, every cartridge then expanding to its bytes unchanged. It enters the engine or the language only on a measurement and a second client. The market generator, the ladder, the picker and the lattice are Buffet's own until another cartridge writes one.
- **The three plates** are Whitworth's (`Frazaro/docs/metameta/METAMETALISP4.md` §2): the cartridge, the game written by hand in Rust (`MISE.2`) and the game written a third time in the page's JavaScript are rubbed against one another until every frame agrees, and no outside standard is needed for that. What the plates cannot tell is whether the game is any good, which is why every course ends with a person at the table.

## The standing decisions: the kitchen's rules

*Declared first, before any dish uses them, for the reason `METAMETALISP4.md` §0 and §1 give: a decision a team runs on but never writes down is re-derived, slightly differently, at every occasion that needs it, and a decision written down expands at every call site whether anyone remembers it or not. Numbered so an item can cite them, each with its reason attached so that a later hand can tell doctrine from accident. `BD-2`, `BD-4` and `BD-6` are the owner's words from `BUFFET.md`; the rest are proposed (rec), to be adopted, amended or struck by the owner at `COURSE.0`'s approval, and until then they bind this file's items and nothing else. The engine's rules cross the road under their own numbers and are never re-minted: `AD-6` (everything the engine holds is a cell), `AD-7` (the step computes values and never formulas), `SD-13` (no outbound call, ever), the `GENSYM` veto, and the kit's ratchet in Alonzo's `ROADMAP.md` under "The order, and why".*

- **`BD-1`. Every course is a game, and a course changes no course before it.** A course is served only when it is playable alone, from the exam to an ending, with its own replay golden, its own describe golden and the owner's hand test; and the course before it stays byte for byte what it was, proved by the pass expanding the earlier course's frozen source to its frozen cartridge (`MISE.1`'s second slice). A course's bytes change only by a dated fix of its own, its replay re-recorded, never by a later course's build. So a playtester plays course 0, then course 1 as an expansion over it, and so on to 7, and every replay ever recorded keeps playing. *Why:* the composition rule is cheap on the day the first course is frozen and dear on any day after, which is the rising-cost test exactly.
- **`BD-2`. Funny is a requirement (owner).** The game is fun insofar as it is funny, jokes all the way down. Every player-visible string is a row of a data sheet with a chance at a joke; the voice is `BUFFET.md` §2's deadpan compliance; the rules are its rules, punch up, fictional names, clarity first, strings as data, PG-13. A string with no joke has a reason written beside it.
- **`BD-3`. The market is exogenous, deterministic and open.** The market's path is a pure function of the seed, the difficulty and the scenario; the dice are a Park and Miller cell seeded from `Clock!B3` and nothing else is random; the player's orders never move a price (slippage is a cost, not a path); and the model is a sheet anyone may read, so the open-book problem of `BUFFET.md` §5 is a feature by design, and a score is alpha over a benchmark on the same seed (`CRITIC.1`), never a raw net worth. *Why:* a replay replays the market, scores on different seeds compare, and nothing has to be hidden from a player who can open every cell (`AD-6`).
- **`BD-4`. Checkers first, chess one license at a time (owner).** The first five minutes need nothing a license has not unlocked; each license is an exam and a layer of the interface; a mechanic that would frighten a first player stands behind a license and never on the opening screen. Progressive disclosure is the product.
- **`BD-5`. Toy models, labeled.** Every simplification is a row of the *Things this game gets wrong* sheet (`MENU.5`); the game is not advice and says so; no real data, no feed, no real ticker's price (`SD-13`); no real money, ever; a concept card is read by a finance educator before it ships, since "tutorial" implies correctness. *Why:* a simplification labeled is a lesson and a simplification hidden is a liability (`BUFFET.md` §12).
- **`BD-6`. Lovelace Lavender and nothing else (owner's standing rule for the house's games).** Gains and losses show by glyph and shade step, never by red and green; a semantic exception for the meaning of loss is the owner's to grant and is an open decision, not a rule.
- **`BD-7`. The market day is the market's clock, and only the calendar reads the frame.** One tick is one market day; prices, fees, timers and events move at a day's boundary and nowhere else; a person's act is answered at the next frame, inside the day. The Day sheet alone reads `Clock!B1` (`POT.1`); every other formula reads the day, so that a frame between boundaries recomputes nothing but what the person touched. Held by the step row's own count, `(step frame evaluated of done)`, the cells an ordinary frame evaluates being the calendar's and what the person touched, and by `describe`'s census showing no shape computed whole each frame. *Why:* the engine computes only what a change reaches (`vla-lang` 0.9.3's `KERNEL.35`), and a model that reads the frame in one cell pays for one cell's change a frame and for the whole market once a day.
- **`BD-8`. State is a cell, and the cartridge never writes code.** A position, a balance, a timer, a license is formula state over its twin (the kit's `count`, `hold` and `since`) or a value the Write sheet puts there (`put`), and never a thing the host keeps (`AD-6`); the cartridge's step writes values and never formulas (`AD-7`); the one place a formula enters at run time is a person's own act on the Strategy sheet (`RECIPE.1`), recorded by the replay as an edit. Strings are data; a picker is `CHOOSE` over references, generated by a macro.
- **`BD-9`. Two pockets, and no switch moves money.** The firm's capital and the partner's wealth are separate cells; the Personal/Firm selector changes which pocket an order funds and what the plate shows, and never a balance; every transfer, draw or contribution is a row of the ledger with the pocket it left and the pocket it entered. *Why:* a selector that moved money would be state the replay could not read off the ledger.
- **`BD-10`. Nothing leaves the kitchen untasted.** The game written by hand in Rust holds every frame of every course (`MISE.2`); where mathematics has a closed form, the cells are held to it to the last bit or a stated tolerance; a course has a replay golden and a describe golden; the playtest pass (Alonzo's `ROADMAP.md`, the owner's name of 2026-10-12) runs before a course is served; an accelerator, a kit hoist or a language ask is made with a measurement in hand and never before. *Why:* Whitworth's plates, above; and `METAMETALISP4.md` §5's field mark, that the real instrument keeps acquiring specimens, which is what a course is.

## The order of service, and why

**The rule.** Work is ranked by its cost of delay, and what is dearest to undo goes first (`METAMETALISP4.md` §3): a layout, a name, a number in a contract, a sheet's address every later formula reads. An instrument earns its place before a dish by the three tests of §5: something named gets dearer each day it is missing (rising cost), the instrument is smaller than the work it unlocks (proportion), and the kitchen is its first user this week (dogfood). An instrument with no specimen this course waits for the course that has one, since the microscope is justified by the dissection and only by it (§8). And the first version of anything is made badly on purpose and improved in use (§2, §Ω): a button before a plate, four rungs as rows before a ladder, one panel before a tab strip, the market moving on the page before a single order can be placed.

**The courses.** The sequence is `BUFFET.md` §13's, kept because each course isolates one cost for the engine and one decision for the player, and what each course builds is what makes the next one cheap:
